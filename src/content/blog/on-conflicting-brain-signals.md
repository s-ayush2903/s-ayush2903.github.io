I do not have a background in biology / neurosciences, hence whatever that I

- **Prefrontal Cortex**: optimizes for longer term pursuit, planning, social reasoning.
- **Limbic system**: optimizes for immediate action, primarily survival instinct

Whenever we are in pain, stress, anxiety, fear, the limbic system signals about
how to survive, what step to take next.

Now it can be thought that why both need to co-exist in the brain? The tension
between them is the feature, not a design compromise. Evolution kept both
so that organism can switch between instinct driven and reason driven behavior
depending upon the context.

A brain without limbic system would have no instinct for survival, no immediate
action on the instant in adversarial situation, thereby failing survival.
A brain without prefrontal cortex would keep seeking immediate gratification,
cannot plan, operate socially at scale. It would also lose.

Now let's talk about decision making. How the decision is made when there are
conflicting signals from different parts of the brain? Your limbic system asking
you to relax due to some distress and the prefrontal cortex's signal to be
consistent. Well, this is quite involved and difficult part. There have been
studies(Libet's experiments in the 1980s showed neural activity preceding conscious awareness of a decision by several hundred milliseconds.)
that show decision has already been made when the signals fire, and it
is the _narrator_ the builds the _convincing story_ when it has to reason about it - confabulation.

Let's talk about both one by one.

## High Level Structure of the Brain
The brain is largely divided into 2 parts, as seen from the top - hemispheres.
It is like a walnut. Those two hemispheres are named as the left and right
hemisphere. The left hemisphere is responsible for controlling the right side of
our body - right hand, right leg, etc. whereas the right hemisphere accounts for
the left side. Language, in most right-handed people, is predominantly handled
by the left hemisphere. The right one is better at spatial processing, pattern
recognition, and holistic processing.

These two hemispheres are connected by a nerve tract - _Corpus Callosum_.
Consider it a really high-bandwidth channel that enables constant information
sharing between the two hemispheres. Vision is split by visual field, not by
eye - everything to the left of your center of focus goes to the right
hemisphere, and everything to the right goes to the left hemisphere. Normally,
the corpus callosum bridges this split so fast that you never notice it.

## Split Brain Research
But what if the two hemispheres are not allowed to communicate? There is a
famous experiment called the _Split Brain Experiment_ where the corpus callosum
is surgically cut. The person sits in front of a screen and is asked to focus on
a dot at the center. A word is flashed briefly on one side of the screen - so
briefly that the eyes cannot move to follow it.

### 1. With the word "Spoon"
The word is flashed on the left side of the screen, so only the right hemisphere
sees it. When asked what they saw, the person says "Nothing" or "I don't know" -
because the language system lives in the left hemisphere, and the left hemisphere
genuinely received no information. The corpus callosum was cut, so nothing
crossed over.

Now the person is verbally instructed to reach into a bag - without looking -
and pick the correct object using their left hand. They pick the spoon correctly.

Two things just happened simultaneously in the same skull:
the right hemisphere knows what it saw and can act on it.
The left hemisphere has no idea and reports nothing.
One brain, two parallel, isolated streams of information.

To summarize:

| Entity          | Hemisphere |
|-----------------|------------|
| Word "spoon"    | Right      |
| Left hand       | Right      |
| Language system | Left       |

### 2. The Walk Experiment
They flash the word "walk" to the right hemisphere only. The person stands up
and starts walking. When asked verbally "Why did you start walking?", the left
hemisphere answers "To stretch my legs" or "I felt like it" - but never "I don't
know." It made up a reason, confidently, in real time. There was no sense of
lying. It felt like a genuine explanation.

_This_ is the _Narrator_. The left hemisphere's language system, when confronted
with a behavior it did not cause and does not understand, automatically generates
a plausible story and presents it as truth.

To summarize:

| Entity              | Hemisphere |
|---------------------|------------|
| Word "walk"         | Right      |
| Left leg / body     | Right      |
| Language system     | Left       |

## Narrator
In a normal brain with an intact corpus callosum, this is happening constantly -
you just cannot catch it, because the real cause and the confabulated story are
both accessible to the same integrated system, so the story feels like memory
rather than invention. The split-brain surgery did not create the narrator. It
created a window through which you can see the narrator operating.

So why does the narrator exist at all? In early times, humans formed social
groups, which meant they had to communicate ideas, share thoughts, and give
explanations. An organism that could explain its own behavior - to others and to
itself - cooperated better, avoided conflict better, and built coalitions better.
"Why did you eat more food?" required an answer. The ones who could narrate
themselves convincingly had social advantages, and so the narrator got selected
for - even though it is not always accurate.

The narrator does not necessarily know the true reason for an action. So it
constructs a story that is coherent and feels true - this is called
_confabulation_. It is not lying. The narrator genuinely believes the story it
generates. And in a normal brain, you have no internal signal that distinguishes
accurate introspection from convincing confabulation. It all feels the same from
the inside.

## Decision Making
The two systems are in constant competitions, they do not take turns. There is
no authoritative entity - arbiter - that decides this particular signal will be
executed. It instead is a weighted contest, the weights are determined by
factors like hunger, fear, emotional state, habits, identity, practiced behavior
and overrides (consider the things that you've determined strictly to NOT do - self-forbidden).
For my morning incident, the prefrontal cortex's signals dominated the ones from
the limbic system's, probably because I've been trying hard to get consistent
and because the distress, while real, was not acute enough to fully override a
4-week streak.

The key point to understand here is that the weights here themselves are
dynamic, change over time. As you can see, hunger is also factored in, now the
decision can vary really differently if the hunger is really high vs it is a
little, like a snack. These weights develop subconsciously and we can
_influence_ them over time, even if we cannot control the outcome of every
single election.
Something that says the narrator obscures the actual mechanism, so let us look at what the mechanism actually is.

## Who is the Co-ordinator?
It is quite intuitive to think that there must be some central authority which
determines the final result, or even the one that keeps the systems in sync -
there is not.

There are many systems in the brain that function independently, they
co-ordinate among themselves with exchange of signals. Consider a distributed
system where nodes interact via a gossip protocol(sharing information with one
another), and they all have the same algorithm for leader election. Now, with
the algorithm they follow the rules specified by it and end up declaring a particular
node as the leader.

The brain is more nuanced than this. It does not work this way, because
decisions need to be context & situation aware. Moreover, there is no stable
quorum, persistent leader state. Each decision is different under different
situations - the context & situation awareness we talked about.


Consider this, instead of having the same version of the
algorithm over all the nodes, all those nodes contain a slightly different
version, dynamic weights that change with context and the situation, some nodes
can go partially offline(prefrontal cortex under extreme fear), no co-ordinator
persists between elections. What emerges now is an action, not a leader.


## Narrator as the false Co-ordinator
After each election, the narrator retrospectively declares that there was a
unified decision maker. It stitches the outcomes to the continuous autobiography
of the sequence of events to craft a plausible explanation of consistent self.
That self is fiction - socially necessary, but not structurally existent.
It is socially necessary to communicate, justify the actions. Imagine a society
where one couldn't justify their actions, it would have become really difficult to survive.
(consider example: "why did you take more food?")


## Closing Thoughts
This system developed and evolved over millions of years, exists within us, and we rarely think about it.
